## Derivation Route: Conditioning on Shared Columns

**Setup:** Partition $X = [X_r \mid X_{a-r} \mid X_{b-r}]$ where $X_r \in \mathbb{C}^{t \times r}$ are the shared columns, $X_{a-r}$ and $X_{b-r}$ are exclusive to $S$ and $T$ respectively. All columns i.i.d. $\mathcal{CN}(0, I_t)$.

**Key conditional independence:** Given $X_r$, the blocks $X_{a-r}$ and $X_{b-r}$ are independent. However, $\det W_S$ and $\det W_T$ are **not** conditionally independent given $X_r$ alone—they share the randomness of $X_r$ in their Schur complements.

### Schur Complement Factorization

$$\det W_S = \det(X_r^H X_r) \cdot \det(X_{a-r}^H P_r^\perp X_{a-r})$$
$$\det W_T = \det(X_r^H X_r) \cdot \det(X_{b-r}^H P_r^\perp X_{b-r})$$

where $P_r^\perp = I - X_r(X_r^H X_r)^{-1}X_r^H$ projects onto the $(t-r)$-dimensional orthogonal complement of $\text{col}(X_r)$.

**Critical measurability point:** $P_r^\perp$ is a measurable function of $X_r$ alone (no arbitrary eigenbasis selection needed—the projection matrix is canonical). The conditional law of $P_r^\perp X_{a-r}$ given $X_r$ is $\mathcal{CN}(0, P_r^\perp)$, which lives in a random subspace but has a **distributionally fixed** nonzero spectrum.

### Conditional Determinant Laws

Given $X_r$:
- $X_{a-r}^H P_r^\perp X_{a-r} \sim \text{Wishart}_{\mathbb{C}}(t-r, a-r)$ — this follows from your already-proved arbitrary column-selected actual Wishart laws applied in the residual space. No global frame needed; the Bartlett decomposition gives this directly.
- Similarly $X_{b-r}^H P_r^\perp X_{b-r} \sim \text{Wishart}_{\mathbb{C}}(t-r, b-r)$
- **These two are conditionally independent given $X_r$** because $X_{a-r} \perp X_{b-r}$ and both interact only through the measurable $P_r^\perp$.

### Computing the Joint Moment

$$E[(\det W_S)^p (\det W_T)^q] = E\left[(\det X_r^H X_r)^{p+q} \cdot E[(\det \tilde{W}_S)^p \mid X_r] \cdot E[(\det \tilde{W}_T)^q \mid X_r]\right]$$

where $\tilde{W}_S, \tilde{W}_T$ are the conditionally independent Schur complement determinants.

By your proved integer determinant moments for complex Wishart:
- $E[(\det X_r^H X_r)^{p+q}] = \prod_{i=0}^{r-1} \frac{\Gamma(t-i+p+q)}{\Gamma(t-i)}$
- $E[(\det \tilde{W}_S)^p \mid X_r] = \prod_{i=r}^{a-1} \frac{\Gamma(t-i+p)}{\Gamma(t-i)}$ (constant, not depending on $X_r$)
- $E[(\det \tilde{W}_T)^q \mid X_r] = \prod_{i=r}^{b-1} \frac{\Gamma(t-i+q)}{\Gamma(t-i)}$

The conditional expectations are **deterministic** (do not depend on $X_r$), so the outer expectation factors completely. This yields exactly your conjectured product.

## Verification: $p=q=1$ Case

Your formula gives:
$$\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(t-i+1)(t-i) \cdot \prod_{i=r}^{a-1}(t-i) \cdot \prod_{i=r}^{b-1}(t-i)$$

For single moments: $E[\det W_S] = \prod_{i=0}^{a-1}(t-i) = t!/(t-a)!$ (falling factorial).

The ratio:
$$\frac{E[\det W_S \det W_T]}{E[\det W_S]E[\det W_T]} = \frac{\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(t-i+1)}{\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(t-i)} = \prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac{t-i+1}{t-i} = \frac{t+1}{t-r+1}$$

This matches your stated $p=q=1$ reduction. ✓

## Determinant Size-Bias Law

**Confirmed.** As unnormalized measures on $\mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$ positive definite:

$$(\det W)^p \cdot K(t-n, n) \det(W)^{t-n} e^{-\text{tr} W} dW = K(t-n,n) \det(W)^{t+p-n} e^{-\text{tr} W} dW$$

$$(\det W)^p \cdot \text{Wishart}(t,n) = \frac{K(t-n,n)}{K(t+p-n,n)} \cdot \text{Wishart}(t+p, n)$$

as weighted measures. This is immediate from the density form you've already proved. No additional work needed.

## Smallest Reusable Next Lemma

> **Lemma (Shared-Column Residual Factorization):** Let $X \in \mathbb{C}^{t \times n}$ have i.i.d. $\mathcal{CN}(0,I_t)$ columns. For any partition into shared/exclusive index sets with $|S \cap T| = r$, conditionally on $X_{S \cap T}$, the Schur complement determinants $\det(W_S)/\det(W_{S\cap T})$ and $\det(W_T)/\det(W_{S\cap T})$ are independent, with laws $\text{det-Wishart}_{\mathbb{C}}(t-r, |S|-r)$ and $\text{det-Wishart}_{\mathbb{C}}(t-r, |T|-r)$ respectively.

**Measurability obligations:**

2. The conditional law statement requires no choice of eigenbasis—only that $P_r^\perp X_{a-r}$ has the same distribution as applying a rank-$(t-r)$ projection to standard Gaussians, which follows from rotational invariance of $\mathcal{CN}(0,I)$
3. Integer moment existence is guaranteed by your proved results (no integrability gaps)

## Doubts and Conditions

1. **No doubt about correctness.** The factorization is exact because the conditional expectations are deterministic—this is special to the determinant (product of all eigenvalues) and would fail for partial spectral functions.

2. **Condition:** Requires $t \geq n$ (given) and that $X_r$ has full column rank a.s. (true since $r \leq n \leq t$ and continuous distribution).

This catches any hidden constant errors.

4. **Not mixing real/complex:** All rising factorials use complex Wishart moments $\Gamma(k+p)/\Gamma(k)$ with $k = t-i$ (integer), no $n+2$ shifts. Verified consistent with your density $K(d,n)\det^d e^{-\text{tr}}$.
