### Step-by-Step Abstract Plan

1. **Understand the Problem**: We need to show that the joint distribution of the energy pair \((\frac{\|Kz\|^2}{2}, \frac{\|K^\perp z\|^2}{2})\) for a random \(z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, E)\) is the product of two independent \(\Gamma(k, 1)\) distributions.

2. **Key Observations**:
   - The energy of a random vector under Gaussian orthogonal projection is \(\frac{1}{2}\|Kz\|^2\) and \(\frac{1}{2}\|K^\perp z\|^2\) are independent.
   - The independence of the two energies is a consequence of the independence of \(K\) and \(K^\perp\) projections.
3. **Formal Proof**:
   - The map \(z \mapsto (\frac{1}{2}\|Kz\|^2, \frac{1}{2}\|K^\perp z\|^2)\) is measurable and its image is a subset of \(\mathbb{R}^2\).
   - The independence of the two components follows from the independence of the projections \(K\) and \(K^\perp\).
4. **Final Step**:
   - The joint distribution of the energy pair is the product of two independent \(\Gamma(k, 1)\) distributions.
