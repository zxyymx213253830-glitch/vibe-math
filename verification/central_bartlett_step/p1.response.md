# Route Comparison: Complex Bartlett Matrix Block

## Route A: Fixed-u Existential Frame + Fubini

- **Hidden assumptions**: The fiber law (z,B)|u depends only on ||u||, not u's direction; the map (u,V)↦(z,B) is jointly measurable; the existential basis doesn't need joint measurability in u.
- **Key lemmas**: `Orthonormal.exists_orthonormalBasis_extension_of_card_eq` (verified) gives a basis per fixed u≠0. Gaussian invariance under real linear isometries (verified) makes the fiber law rotation-invariant. Skew-product (verified) disintegrates over r=||u||².
- **Likely failure**: **Legitimacy gap.** Without a jointly measurable frame selection u↦e₁(u), you cannot directly push forward the joint law through (u,V)↦(z,B). Fubini requires the fiber map to be measurable. The existential basis lets you prove "for each u, the conditional law is Wishart(m-1,n)," but gluing these fibers into a joint statement demands either (i) a measurable selector or (ii) showing the fiber law is constant a.e. and invoking the skew-product abstractly. Option (ii) works *if* you prove the conditional distribution of B given u is actually independent of u/||u|| — this is where Gaussian rotation invariance must do the heavy lifting, applied to the *map definition*, not just pointwise fibers.
- **Check type**: Does the skew-product theorem require only that the fiber measure is a.e. constant, or does it need an explicit measurable family? If the former, route A is legitimate without a measurable basis.
- **Zero-u set**: Measure-zero since r~Gamma(m,1), m≥1.

## Route B: Measurable Householder with Patches

- **Hidden assumptions**: A phase convention for complex Householder reflections can be made globally measurable; the exception set (u∝e₁) is handled without breaking measurability.
- **Key lemmas**: Explicit construction of a measurable unitary U(u) with U(u)e₁=u/||u||, patched on a null set. [Unverified: no mathlib complex Householder.]
- **Likely failure**: Engineering burden. Complex Householder H=I-(1-e^{iθ})wwᴴ requires choosing θ(u) measurably. Phase discontinuities are topologically obstructed on ℂᵐ\{0}. Patching works measure-theoretically but adds ~200 lines of boilerplate.
- **Check type**: Constructive measurability proof.
- **Core size**: Largest.

## Route C: Linear Projection / Residual Independence

- **Hidden assumptions**: None about bases. Uses only that P_u = uuᴴ/||u||² is a measurable orthogonal projector and V - P_u V is the residual.
- **Key lemmas**: For jointly Gaussian (u,V), the projection P_u V and residual (I-P_u)V are conditionally independent given u, with covariances computable from P_u. z = uᴴV/||u|| is exactly the coordinate along P_u. B = Vᴴ(I-P_u)V after centering. Gaussian invariance under real isometries (verified) shows (I-P_u)V has the same law as an (m-1)-dimensional standard Gaussian projected onto im(P_u)^⊥, **without naming a basis**.
- **Likely failure**: Showing B's conditional law is exactly Wishart(m-1,n) requires identifying im(P_u)^⊥ ≅ ℂ^{m-1} isometrically. But you only need the *distribution* of the Gram matrix of n i.i.d. CN(0,I_{m-1}) vectors, which is basis-free: it's the pushforward through any isometry, and all such pushforwards agree.
- **Check type**: Covariance computation + uniqueness of Gaussian measures.
- **Core size**: Smallest.

## Verdict

**Route C is smallest core.** It avoids measurable basis selection entirely by working with the projector P_u directly. Route A *can* avoid measurable basis selection legitimately **only if** your skew-product theorem accepts a.e.-constant fiber measures without requiring a measurable trivialization — check this precisely. If it demands explicit families, A inherits B's engineering cost. Route C's key insight: B = Vᴴ(I-P_u)V is manifestly measurable, and its law follows from the fact that (I-P_u)V|u is a rank-(m-1) Gaussian whose Gram matrix law is unique regardless of which isometry identifies im(P_u)^⊥ with ℂ^{m-1}. Zero-u is null in all routes.
